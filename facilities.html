Facilities

A vendor that calls the unit back.

Discharge planners do not need another app login. They need a person who can hear “the floor is two hours late” and change the van without losing the rider. This page is for hospitals, skilled nursing, rehab, and dialysis clinics.

Send this once

  • Rider name, callback, and whether a caregiver is traveling.
  • Mobility: manual chair, power chair, scooter, walker, ambulatory, or must stay flat.
  • Chair width if it is a power base, and whether a bariatric van is required.
  • Pickup entrance and unit, not only the campus name.
  • Who receives the rider at the destination, and a number that is answered.
  • Stairs at home, and whether anyone will be there.
  • Billing contact if this is a facility account rather than family private-pay.
  • Whether the return is today, another day, or not your problem to book.

What comes back

  • A direct desk number during 5:30a–8:00p, and an after-8 line only for trips already booked.
  • A written confirmation with vehicle type before the driver is assigned.
  • A call when the release window slips, with a revised time, not a silent no-show.
  • An invoice per trip, or a monthly statement for accounts that run more than a few rides.
  • A straight no when the trip is clinical, uncovered geographically, or the board is full.

Send a facility request

How accounts stay small on purpose

We would rather be the reliable second call for East Bay discharges than a logo on a regional brochure. Accounts start with a named coordinator on your side and a named dispatcher on ours. Standing dialysis schedules are welcome. A promise of “any bed, any hour, any county” is not.

Private-pay families can book without a facility account. If a health plan is the payer, we are the wrong vendor — we do not bill Medi-Cal or Medicare, and we will not dress up a private invoice as if we did.
