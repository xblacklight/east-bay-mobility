Services

The vehicle changes. The door does not.

People call this non-emergency medical transportation. On our board it is simpler: a confirmed ride for someone who should not be driving, transferring alone, or waiting at a curb they cannot see from the lobby.

01

Wheelchair and scooter

The chair you already use is the seat. Manual chairs, most power bases, and scooters ride in a lift van with a four-point tie-down and a lap belt. We ask the chair’s width, whether it tilts or reclines, and whether the rider can stay upright for the trip.

If a transfer into a captain’s chair would be safer, we say that before the day of the ride. We do not decide it in the driveway. A power base that is too wide for the van we have that morning is a no, not a squeeze.

02

Companion rides

For riders who walk, including with a cane or walker, and should not drive themselves home. The driver walks at your pace from the door to the seat and into the destination lobby. Useful after a procedure, for a first visit to a campus, or when the family car is with someone who is still at work.

One caregiver rides along when the vehicle has a belt. A second person is a question for the quote, not an assumption.

Request a companion ride

03

Dialysis, as a route

Dialysis is not a one-off errand. Chair times repeat, mornings are early, and the ride home starts when treatment ends, which is rarely the minute on the printout. We build a standing order around that: default door, default clinic, a driver who already knows the chair, and a return window instead of a fantasy timestamp.

Families can leave the standing order alone and call only when the chair time, the door, or the rider’s mobility changes. If a health plan is supposed to provide this ride, use that benefit first. We are a private-pay desk.

Set up a standing ride

04

Discharge and transfers

Mileage is the easy part. The release window, the entrance, and the person receiving the rider are the job. Tell us the unit, the case manager’s direct number, and whether the destination can take the rider across a range of time. We call when the floor is running late instead of leaving a driver invisible in a garage.

Sending a rider home to an empty house, or to stairs we were not told about, is how discharges fail. Those two facts belong on the first call.

For facilities

05

Stairs, said plainly

A lot of East Bay houses have a flight between the sidewalk and the front door and no elevator. A short flight, a rail, and a rider who can bear some weight can be a two-person assist we schedule on purpose.

A long flight, no rail, or a rider who cannot bear weight is not something we invent a way through on arrival. We will say whether we can staff it or whether you need a lift team we do not run that day. Guessing at the curb is how people get hurt.

Describe the stairs

06

Outside the job

We do not replace an ambulance. We do not manage oxygen, monitor symptoms, or carry a nurse. If the rider must stay flat, ask about a gurney van — some days a second attendant is on the board, and some days the honest answer is to call a provider who does stretcher transport as their whole job.

We also do not bill Medi-Cal or Medicare. If a plan covers non-emergency transportation, that line is the right first call. East Bay Paratransit is the public ADA service for people who cannot use AC Transit or BART, and it has its own eligibility. We will say so when that is the better fit.

Bring the mobility, the campus, and the time.

If you are missing one of those, the form will still let you write what you know. The desk would rather have a partial truth than a complete guess.
